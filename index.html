6 Exploring an X1.8 flare and associated quasi-periodic pulsations using SDO and Aditya-L1 observations, alongside NFFF extrapolation

Pawan Kumar, Sanjay Kumar, Sadashiv, Sachin Rajkapoor Kanaujiya et al. · submitted 2026-10-06 direct
21 pages, 9 figures accepted for publication in Solar Physics
arXiv:2610.08124PDFADS

Case study of the X1.8 flare SOL2024-10-09T01:24 (AR 13848). It detects ~4-min QPPs in the HEL1OS/Aditya-L1 20–50 keV lightcurve, localises them with STIX MEM_GE imaging and AIA ribbon lightcurves to one ribbon (R1), and interprets them via NFFF-extrapolated null/flux-rope/QSL topology.

The authors combine GOES-18 SXR, AIA EUV/UV imaging, HEL1OS 20–50 keV HXR lightcurves (2 s bins), a single STIX MEM_GE image, and an NFFF extrapolation of the HMI SHARP magnetogram at 01:24 UT. Method: they detrend the HXR lightcurve with a Savitzky–Golay filter (251 s window), then apply a Morlet wavelet (Torrence & Compo) and an FFT periodogram tested against an AR(1) red-noise 95% level. Both give a period of ~4 min (4.14 min FFT, 3.94 min global wavelet) during the impulsive phase, ~01:35–01:50 UT. AIA 171 Å and 1600 Å lightcurves of four ribbons/brightenings show that peaks from ribbon R1 roughly coincide with the HXR peaks. A STIX 20–50 keV image (01:42:00–01:42:30 UT), reprojected to the Earth view and manually co-aligned, overlaps R1. The extrapolation shows a flux rope (|Tw|>1), a 3D null, a sheared arcade and four QSLs. The authors propose that recurrent reconnection at the null and in the arcade drives both flare onset and the QPPs, but they acknowledge this cannot explain the period itself.

Connections

  • Directly on topic for QPPs in flare HXR and their spatial localisation in ribbons. It follows the Clarke et al. 2021 and Collier et al. 2024 approach of correlating ribbon UV/EUV lightcurves with HXR pulsations (Fig. 6a).
  • Uses STIX data through stixpy with MEM_GE imaging and SunPy reprojection to the Earth view (Sect. 2.2, Fig. 6b). This is the same toolchain the PhD will use, though applied only minimally here.
  • Supplies a candidate event (2024-10-09 X1.8) whose STIX timing data were not analysed. STIX's own lightcurves and spectra could be used to test the 4-min QPP and its energy dependence.
  • The QPP pipeline (SG detrending → wavelet + FFT with red-noise test) is a common baseline. It is also an example of the detrending pitfalls (Inglis/Hayes, Broomhall et al.) the researcher should avoid or improve on.

Possible applications

  • Re-analyse SOL2024-10-09 X1.8 with STIX quicklook and science lightcurves (4 s or better cadence, multiple energy bands). This would confirm or refute the 4-min period without detrending, e.g. with a Fourier power-law + Lorentzian fit or the Inglis & Hayes 2024 method, and would test energy-dependent timing and thermal/nonthermal separation.
  • Check Solar Orbiter's position and whether EUI/FSI or HRI observed AR 13848 on 2024-10-09, to localise pulsations in EUV at higher cadence than AIA, which saturated.
  • Do time-resolved STIX imaging across individual pulses rather than a single 30 s window, to see whether footpoints migrate between R1 and other ribbons from pulse to pulse.
  • Run time-resolved STIX spectral fitting (thermal + thick-target) per pulse to track whether the spectral index hardens or softens through the QPPs.
  • Cross-compare HEL1OS and STIX lightcurves for this event as a cross-calibration and timing check.

Caveats

  • The QPP detection is weak methodologically. A 251 s SG detrending window is close to the claimed ~240 s period, which risks artificially imposing or suppressing that period. The pulsations shown are dominated by 2–3 large spikes rather than a clear train of more than three pulses.
  • No energy-resolved timing, no spectral analysis, and only one energy band (20–50 keV) are used.
  • STIX is used only for a single 30 s image with manual co-alignment. STIX lightcurves and spectroscopy are not exploited, and EUI is not used at all.
  • The EUV/UV–HXR correlation is qualitative ('nearly coincident' peaks) with no cross-correlation coefficients or lags. The 171 Å channel is used because other AIA channels saturated.
  • The link between the extrapolated topology and the QPPs is speculative, and the extrapolation is a single static pre-flare snapshot. The authors admit it cannot account for the period.
  • Data are available only on request.

Abstract screen 8/10: Directly addresses QPPs (about 4 min period) in the hard X-ray lightcurve of an X1.8 flare, with EUV ribbon and brightening morphology and a magnetic-reconnection interpretation. It uses SDO and Aditya-L1 rather than STIX/EUI, but the QPP detection methods and physics transfer.

6 First results from PADRE/MeDDEA: X-rays from a flare and its CME

Muriel Zoë Stiefel, Säm Krucker, Olivier Limousin, Niharika Godbole et al. · submitted 2026-09-28 instrument
accepted in Astronomy & Astrophysics, 9 pages, 6 figures, 1 table
arXiv:2609.34999PDFADS

First science paper from PADRE/MeDDEA (a CubeSat flying STIX flight-spare CdTe detectors). It cross-calibrates MeDDEA against Fermi/GBM and ASO-S/HXI to within ~10% at 18–30 keV for the M6 flare SOL2025-11-28, then uses STIX's 15°-occulted view to show the CME-associated HXR source carries ~1% of the flare nonthermal photon flux but comparable thermal energy.

The paper analyses SOL2025-11-28T22 (GOES M6), which was on the limb from Earth and occulted by ~15° (24.5 Mm occultation height) for Solar Orbiter/STIX. Six HXR instruments are compared through spectrograms (Fig. 1) and lightcurves (Fig. 2). This required a 5 s timing correction for MeDDEA and a +48.3 s light-travel-time shift for STIX. MeDDEA 0.5 s cadence 28–70 keV lightcurves match GBM and HXI. Spectral fitting covers 8 s at the nonthermal peak, using OSPEX for STIX/HXI/GBM and sunkit-spex for MeDDEA, with an isothermal plus thick-target model (Fig. 3). Results: - MeDDEA/HXI/GBM agree to 1.01±0.10 at 18–30 keV (Fig. 4). - The occulted STIX coronal/CME source has the same δ≈5.25 as the flare (δ=5.22) at 0.95±0.15% of its flux. - The occulted source is cooler (12.9 MK vs 29.4 MK). - Its thermal energy is comparable to the flare's (Table 1). STIX CLEAN images (sub-collimators 7–10) combined with AIA 131 Å show the source moving outward at ~450 km/s with the erupting flux rope (Fig. 6). The occulted lightcurve shows slower decay and a Neupert-like thermal rise during the nonthermal burst. A stopping-column-density argument (~1.7×10^9 cm^-3 needed vs ~2×10^9 inferred) mildly favours thick-target emission in the CME source.

Connections

  • Directly uses STIX spectroscopy and imaging with the latest calibration (Massa et al. 2026, sswidl v0.6.2). The OSPEX workflow following Krucker & Masuda (2026) is a template for STIX spectral fitting in the PhD.
  • Shows sunkit-spex used for thick-target plus thermal forward fitting of a STIX-like detector, cross-validated against OSPEX (Sec. 2.2). This is the Python/SunPy-ecosystem spectral tool the project would use.
  • Timing caveats relevant to multi-instrument energy-dependent timing studies: the STIX light-travel-time correction of +48.3 s and the MeDDEA clock offset of 5 s, found by peak matching (Sec. 3.1).
  • Occulted versus on-disk lightcurve comparison (Fig. 2): the coronal nonthermal source is less impulsive, has a longer decay, and shows a Neupert-like thermal rise. This is a temporal diagnostic of trapping and energy loss in coronal sources that is relevant to electron transport.
  • Thermal energy estimates via Eth=3kT√(EM·V) with 50%-contour volumes, and thick- versus thin-target density arguments (Sec. 3.3, Sec. 4). These are reusable energetics methods.
  • Explicitly notes EUI was at 6-min cadence and missed the impulsive phase. This is a practical reminder that EUI cadence must be checked when selecting events for STIX/EUI timing work.

Possible applications

  • Use MeDDEA (same SO-Caliste detectors as STIX) as an Earth-view counterpart for STIX flares. Possible uses are stereoscopic timing, directivity checks, or validating STIX lightcurves and spectra.
  • Adopt the sunkit-spex fitting workflow for time-resolved STIX spectroscopy (e.g., evolution of δ and T through impulsive bursts and QPP peaks).
  • Build a sample of STIX-occulted / Earth-on-disk events to compare coronal versus footpoint nonthermal time profiles and decay times. These constrain trapping and transport.
  • Check SOL2025-11-28 MeDDEA 0.5 s 28–70 keV lightcurves for short-timescale structure or pulsations. The impulsive peak in Fig. 2 shows several sub-peaks, though this is not analysed in the paper.

Caveats

  • Focus is instrument validation and flare-CME energetics, not QPPs or energy-dependent timing. There is no time-resolved spectral evolution, only one 8 s spectrum.
  • The event was occulted for STIX and EUI data were not used (6-min cadence), so it gives no STIX/EUI ribbon-loop variability information.
  • Single event. Thermal-energy comparisons assume a filling factor of 1 and exclude volume systematics. The thick- versus thin-target choice for the CME source is unresolved.
  • Cross-calibration is validated only at 18–30 keV, due to poor statistics above 30 keV and HXI/GBM calibration issues at lower energies.

5 A New Framework for Modeling Solar Flares from MHD to Kinetic Processes

Joel C. Allred, Graham S. Kerr, Silvina E. Guidoni, Joel T. Dahlin et al. · submitted 2026-10-01 context
21 pages, 12 figures, accepted for publication by Astrophysical Journal
arXiv:2610.02149PDFADS

Chains 2.5D ARMS MHD (breakout eruption, flare current sheet), kglobal (reconnection-driven electron/proton spectra, κ≈2.9) and multi-species RADYN+FP (transport, return current, mirroring) to simulate one flare loop's response to a single 20 s exponentially decaying beam injection. The model predicts evaporation, condensation, white-light continuum and 30–50 keV HXR emission heights.

ARMS provides the current-sheet conditions and a newly reconnected loop. kglobal produces nonthermal electron and proton power laws (κ≈2.9, with most acceleration within ~24 s). These are injected via a leaky-box exponential with τi = 20 s, a value chosen explicitly to match typical QPP periods from Hayes et al. 2020, into a RADYN+FP loop (11 Mm half-length). FP was extended to carry electrons and protons together, coupled through the return current. Key results: - An early return-current Ohmic flash heats the looptop to ~24–28 MK within ~1 s. - Evaporation starts at 1.7 Mm/s and decays to 160 km/s by 40 s; condensation flows are 10–30 km/s. - Protons undergo warm-target collisions in the corona and dump their energy at the transition region. - High pitch-angle electrons are reversed first by the return current and later by the chromospheric magnetic mirror. - The return current is initially carried mostly by reflected nonthermal electrons. - The 5000 Å continuum shows an initial ~5% dimming, then enhancements from 5% (disk centre) to ~400% (limb). - 30–50 keV HXR emissivity is co-spatial with the white-light source at ~690 km, consistent with Krucker et al. 2015. There is no observational analysis, no QPP or timing analysis, and no STIX/EUI data. Only one injection episode is modelled.

Connections

  • Sec 3.3 / Fig 4: injection decay time τi = 20 s is tied to QPP periods (Hayes et al. 2020) and the bursty-reconnection interpretation (Ashfield et al. 2026). This sets up a framework in which each QPP burst = one injection episode, directly relevant to interpreting STIX QPPs, though only one burst is simulated.
  • Fig 2 / Sec 3.2: kglobal predicts κ≈2.9 power laws for a guide field of 0.3, with acceleration over ~5τA ≈ 24 s. This gives a testable spectral-index and acceleration-timescale prediction against STIX thick-target fits; the authors note it is harder than typical flares.
  • Sec 3.3.1, Figs 9–11: return-current losses dominate during the first ~0.5 s and then fade as the corona heats. This implies time-dependent low-energy flattening and energy-dependent transport effects that could appear in high-cadence STIX spectral evolution early in each burst.
  • Fig 12 right: 30–50 keV HXR emissivity peaks at ~690 km, co-spatial with the white-light source. This is relevant to STIX footpoint height or limb studies, especially with Solar Orbiter's off-Earth vantage point.
  • Sec 3.3: the coronal temperature evolution (24→40→18 MK) and density evolution (1e8→4e10 cm^-3) give model thermal-source behaviour to compare with STIX thermal fits and EUI/AIA loop brightening timescales.
  • Proton warm-target transport and the momentum push of the transition region give context for ion contributions to footpoint heating not seen in HXR.

Possible applications

  • Forward-model STIX count spectra or lightcurves from the RADYN+FP loop (thick-target bremsstrahlung, already computed for 30–50 keV) and compare thermal/nonthermal timing with observed impulsive bursts.
  • Request or construct multi-burst injection runs (repeated τi ≈ 20 s episodes) to test whether QPP-like modulation in STIX nonthermal and thermal channels and EUI footpoint emission reproduces observed phase lags.
  • Compare observed STIX nonthermal spectral indices and their time evolution in Major Flare Campaign events with the κ≈2.9 prediction and its guide-field dependence.
  • Use the predicted early return-current-dominated phase (<1 s) as a target for STIX's fastest-cadence spectral analysis of very impulsive onsets.
  • Combine STIX footpoint heights from limb or stereoscopic Solar Orbiter–Earth geometries with the ~400–700 km prediction.

Caveats

  • Purely theoretical. No STIX, EUI or other observational data, and no specific flare events.
  • Only a single exponential injection episode into a single 2.5D-derived symmetric loop. No QPPs or temporal variability are actually modelled; the 20 s timescale is an assumption borrowed from QPP statistics.
  • Heavy rescaling: the ARMS active region field is ~2.5 G and the region is ~10× too large, with time scaled by a factor of 40. kglobal uses periodic boundary conditions and Fermi acceleration only.
  • No full X-ray spectra, lightcurves or EUV synthetic observables comparable to EUI are presented; only 5000 Å continuum and a 30–50 keV emissivity profile.
  • The FP code omits runaway return-current electrons during the early Dreicer-exceeding phase, and the time-independent transport assumption fails for protons early on.
Instruments, events, abstract screen

Abstract screen 7/10: It presents an MHD-to-kinetic flare modeling chain (ARMS, kglobal, RADYN+FP) covering electron and proton acceleration, transport and lower-atmosphere heating. That gives the physical context for interpreting STIX/EUI flare timing and particle acceleration, but it is a modeling paper rather than an observational or timing/QPP analysis.
