Plasma Heating and Energization in Hot-Onset Flare Precursor Events

H. Da, J. F. Drake, M. Swisdak
Submitted 10 Sep 2026, astro-ph.SR
H. Da et al 2026 ApJL 1009 L20
arXivPDFADSDOI

2D kglobal (MHD + guiding-centre macroparticle) reconnection simulations with a spatially varying guide field (0.75 near the sheet dropping to 0.2 outside) show an early phase of thermal heating to roughly 12.5 MK with a steadily rising emission measure and few nonthermal particles, followed by rapid nonthermal electron/proton production once the weak-guide-field flux reaches the X-line. The authors offer this as an explanation for the observed flare 'hot onset'.

The paper targets the 'hot onset': the interval before the impulsive phase in which plasma is above 10 MK and emission measure rises steadily with no detectable hard X-rays (Hudson et al. 2020; Battaglia et al. 2023 with STIX). Using kglobal on a 4096×2048 periodic domain, the authors build the guide field so that a strong-to-weak transition over time is reproduced spatially. Strong guide field suppresses Fermi reflection at island ends, giving spatially extended heating (Te roughly doubles; Fig. 4a) and a monotonically rising EM (Fig. 4c), with a low 'hot particle fraction' (Fig. 4b). After t/τA≈14.5 the curvature-drift (Fermi) heating term jumps (Fig. 3), the energetic-particle fraction rises sharply, and electron spectra extend to about 90 keV (Fig. 5a). Electrons gain less than protons because a self-generated parallel electric field traps them and opposes their heating (Fig. 3a), whereas it adds to proton heating. This is pure simulation with no observational analysis. Its value to the researcher is as a physical framework and a testable prediction: the delay between thermal (low-energy STIX) and nonthermal (higher-energy STIX) onset should track a guide-field/shear transition visible in EUV loop tilt.

AbstractRecent observations of solar flares have revealed a distinct 'hot onset' phase preceding the impulsive phase, character…

Recent observations of solar flares have revealed a distinct 'hot onset' phase preceding the impulsive phase, characterized by elevated plasma temperature (>10 MK), a steady increase in emission measure, and the absence of detectable nonthermal emission. The standard flare model attributes the formation of hot flare plasma to chromospheric evaporation driven by energy deposition from nonthermal electrons, and therefore does not naturally explain why thermal heating precedes nonthermal emission. Motivated by recent observations of guide-field evolution in two-ribbon flares and by large-scale MHD simulations, we investigate hot-onset energization using simulations of magnetic reconnection with the kinetic macroscale model kglobal initialized with a spatially non-uniform guide field. Because the efficiency of electron non-thermal energy production from Fermi acceleration decreases with guide-field strength, the evolving guide field produces two distinct energization regimes. During the initial high-guide-field phase, the bulk electron temperature rises to approximately twice its initial value, while non-thermal particle production remains weak. Upon entering the low-guide-field regime, energetic particle production is significantly enhanced, with the population of high-energy electrons growing rapidly. These findings provide a self-consistent physical explanation for the hot onset, demonstrating that the macroscopic evolution of the guide field naturally bridges the hot-onset and impulsive phases of solar flares without requiring a separate trigger mechanism.

  • A physical interpretation for STIX hot-onset intervals: strong guide field gives heating without nonthermal tails; weakening guide field gives impulsive-phase acceleration (Sections 1, 3; Fig. 4).
  • A predicted temperature of about 12.5 MK and a steadily rising EM during hot onset, from Eq. 1 normalisation with B=60 G and n=1e10 cm^-3 (Fig. 4a,c), to compare against STIX thermal fits.
  • Heating-term decomposition showing curvature-drift (Fermi) dominance and the parallel electric field acting against electrons, which bears on electron escape/trapping interpretations (Eq. 3, Fig. 3).
  • Domain-integrated electron spectra showing tail hardening and cutoff growth to about 90 keV after the guide-field transition (Fig. 5a), a qualitative analogue for time-resolved STIX nonthermal fits.
  • An observational proxy pointer: post-reconnection flare-loop tilt as a guide-field diagnostic (Qiu et al. 2023), applicable to EUI/AIA imaging.

Evidence

Fig. 4b and Section 3: the energetic-particle fraction stays low from t/τA≈4.5 to 14.5 and then rises rapidly once the low-guide-field flux reaches the X-line. Electron temperature rises only modestly over the same interval.

Inference

The model predicts that the start of nonthermal HXR emission is set by the macroscopic shear evolution rather than by a separate trigger. The thermal-to-nonthermal delay in STIX lightcurves would then be a proxy for how long the guide field takes to decay.

Recommendation

In STIX case studies and statistics, measure the hot-onset duration (thermal rise to first nonthermal emission). Compare it with EUI/AIA-derived PRFL tilt or ribbon shear evolution to test the prediction.

Caveats5Purely numerical 2D study with no observational data analysis. The time evolution of the guide field is impos…
  • Purely numerical 2D study with no observational data analysis. The time evolution of the guide field is imposed through a prescribed spatial profile rather than arising self-consistently.
  • The temperature in physical units (12.5 MK) and the 90 keV electron cutoff depend on the assumed B and n (Eq. 1); agreement with observations is partly a consequence of parameter choice.
  • No collisional equilibration is included; the 'equilibrium' curve is a post hoc average. The heating increment (about ×2) is below the observed value.
  • The 'hot particle fraction' (energy > 3× late-time thermal energy) is only a rough nonthermal proxy. No synthetic HXR or EUV emission is produced, so direct STIX/EUI comparison is not possible.
  • Nothing is said about QPPs, sub-second variability or energy-dependent timing; island merging is shown but not linked to periodic modulations.
