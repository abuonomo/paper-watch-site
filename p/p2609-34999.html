82.9 First results from PADRE/MeDDEA: X-rays from a flare and its CME

Muriel Zoë Stiefel, Säm Krucker, Olivier Limousin, Niharika Godbole et al. · submitted 2026-09-28 complementary data
accepted in Astronomy & Astrophysics, 9 pages, 6 figures, 1 table
arXiv:2609.34999PDFADS

First science paper from the PADRE/MeDDEA CubeSat spectrometer, which carries STIX flight-spare CdTe detectors: it cross-calibrates MeDDEA against ASO-S/HXI and Fermi/GBM to within ~10% (18–30 keV) for the M6 flare SOL2025-11-28T22, and uses STIX's 15°-occulted view to show that CME-associated nonthermal HXR flux is ~1% of the flare flux while its thermal energy is comparable.

The authors analyse the first large flare seen by MeDDEA (GOES M6, 28 Nov 2025). From Earth the flare was on the limb but visible; for Solar Orbiter/STIX it was occulted by 15° (occultation height 24.5 Mm). Spectral fitting with sunkit-spex (MeDDEA) and OSPEX (STIX, HXI, GBM) gives thermal-plus-thick-target fits. MeDDEA photon fluxes agree with HXI and GBM to 1.01±0.10 in 18–30 keV, after an ad hoc 5.0 s clock correction. The occulted STIX source has a nonthermal flux 0.95%±0.15% of the flare flux, the same spectral index (δ≈5.2), a cooler temperature (~13 MK vs ~29 MK) and comparable thermal energy (~4–6×10^29 vs 2.4×10^29 erg, filling factor 1). STIX CLEAN images (sub-collimators 7–10) show the source moving at ~450 km/s with the AIA 131 Å erupting flux rope. The occulted light curve is less impulsive and decays more slowly, and its thermal emission rises during the nonthermal burst in a Neupert-like way. For the researcher, the value lies in the STIX spectral/imaging workflow, the multi-instrument timing comparison, and a new STIX-cross-calibrated, high-cadence, Earth-view HXR data source with Python tooling. QPPs and fine timing are not addressed.

Same scientific problem2×0.30

The paper is about flare electron energetics and HXR emission from flare versus CME. It touches electron acceleration and transport but not rapid variability, QPPs or energy-dependent timing.

Same methodology3×0.25

It uses STIX spectral fitting (thermal + thick target), STIX CLEAN/MEM imaging, light-travel-time correction and inter-instrument light-curve comparison, with Python sunkit-spex fitting. These methods transfer directly, though no time-series or periodicity methods are used.

Same data / instruments4×0.15

STIX is central: occulted spectra, images and light curves, plus AIA, HXI and GBM. It introduces MeDDEA, which carries STIX-identical detectors, gives 0.5 s cadence and offers an open Python pipeline.

Same physical phenomenon3×0.20

The subject is nonthermal electrons and thermal plasma in a large flare, the Neupert-like heating of the high coronal source and decay timescales set by trapping and conduction. The focus on CME-associated emission is somewhat off-centre.

Potential future utility3×0.10

MeDDEA gives an Earth-view STIX-like spectrometer for stereoscopic timing and spectral comparison of flares, especially occulted ones. Its high-cadence event lists could support timing or QPP work, and the occulted-flare approach can be applied across the STIX catalogue.

Findings53 high · 2 medium confidence
high If MeDDEA is used for timing comparisons with STIX, align it on a reference instrument (GBM/HXI) and check the status of the MeDDEA clock c…
Evidence

The MeDDEA 28–70 keV light curve at 0.5 s cadence has ~9% errors at peak. A 5.0 s clock shift was required to align MeDDEA with HXI and GBM nonthermal peaks (Sect. 3.1, Fig. 2).

Inference

MeDDEA can supply sub-second nonthermal light curves for M-class flares. Its absolute timing is not yet reliable, so any cross-instrument timing analysis (e.g. STIX–MeDDEA lags) would need independent clock verification.

Recommendation

If MeDDEA is used for timing comparisons with STIX, align it on a reference instrument (GBM/HXI) and check the status of the MeDDEA clock correction before interpreting second-level delays.

high Compare in photon space or forward-model through each instrument's response when doing multi-instrument energy-dependent timing.
Evidence

The HXI 10–20 keV curve peaks and decays before MeDDEA's because HXI's thicker entrance window shifts its mean photon energy higher. The thermal HXR curves peak before GOES/XRS (Sect. 3.1).

Inference

Apparent timing differences between instruments in nominally identical energy bands can come from instrument response rather than physics. This matters for energy-dependent timing studies that combine STIX with other instruments.

Recommendation

Compare in photon space or forward-model through each instrument's response when doing multi-instrument energy-dependent timing.

medium Consider occulted STIX events with Earth-view HXR coverage as a way to isolate coronal-source temporal behaviour from ribbon emission.
Evidence

The occulted STIX 12–25 keV profile has a similar onset but is less impulsive and decays more slowly than the flare. The occulted 4–12 keV emission rises during the nonthermal burst, Neupert-like. This is interpreted as trapping, with conductive losses weaker in the high corona (Sect. 3.1, Fig. 2).

Inference

Comparing occulted and unocculted temporal profiles can separate coronal-source variability from footpoint variability. That is relevant to locating where flare variability (including any QPPs) originates.

Recommendation

Consider occulted STIX events with Earth-view HXR coverage as a way to isolate coronal-source temporal behaviour from ribbon emission.

medium Treat the 1% fraction as thick-target-specific. When fitting coronal STIX sources, test both thick- and thin-target models.
Evidence

The occulted nonthermal flux is ~1% of the flare flux, with identical δ≈5.2 in the thick-target fit. The thick- versus thin-target choice is argued from a density estimate (~2×10^9 cm^-3) and is left unresolved (Sect. 3.3, 4).

Inference

This is a quantitative constraint on how the electron population is partitioned between flare and eruption. The interpretation is model-dependent, especially for thin-target or coronal sources.

Recommendation

Treat the 1% fraction as thick-target-specific. When fitting coronal STIX sources, test both thick- and thin-target models.

high Screen candidate events for EUI high-cadence modes before building STIX–EUI timing case studies.
Evidence

EUI was at 6-minute cadence and missed the impulsive phase, so it was excluded (Sect. 3.4).

Inference

EUI cadence during non-campaign periods can limit joint STIX/EUI timing studies. Event selection needs to check EUI cadence, for example by favouring Major Flare Campaign periods.

Recommendation

Screen candidate events for EUI high-cadence modes before building STIX–EUI timing case studies.

Caveats6Single-event study: no QPP, periodicity or energy-dependent timing analysis is performed.
  • Single-event study: no QPP, periodicity or energy-dependent timing analysis is performed.
  • The MeDDEA clock needed an ad hoc 5 s correction, and cross-calibration is only validated in 18–30 keV.
  • Thermal energies assume a filling factor of 1 and ignore systematic volume uncertainties. The volume estimates for the CME source differ by a factor of 2.
  • The 1% electron fraction rests on the thick-target assumption.
  • EUI data were not used, so there is no direct STIX–EUI connection.
  • The CME focus is partly outside the researcher's core flare-variability interests.
