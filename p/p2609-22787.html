A uniform EEMD-plus-wavelet survey of 34 X-class flares (Jul 2024–Mar 2026) in Aditya-L1/HEL1OS 8–20 keV light curves finds QPPs in 25 events (~74%), with periods clustered at 1.3–1.7 min that do not change with energy and modulation depth that grows with photon energy.

SummaryThe authors split HEL1OS light curves in four bands (8–11, 11–14, 14–17, 17–20 keV) into components with Ensemble Empir…

The authors split HEL1OS light curves in four bands (8–11, 11–14, 14–17, 17–20 keV) into components with Ensemble Empirical Mode Decomposition (PyEMD; 10,000 trials, noise width 0.2). They test the selected intrinsic mode function (IMF) against an AR(1) red-noise model using a Morlet wavelet at 95% confidence. Period, duration, quality factor and modulation depth are then measured only inside the wavelet-significant interval. GOES/XRS is analysed the same way for a thermal comparison. Mean periods are about 1.6–1.7 min in every band (Table 1, Fig. 4a). Mean modulation depth rises from 1.3% to 3.8% between 8–11 and 17–20 keV, which the authors fit with a power-law index of about 1.5; GOES depths are only about 0.4–0.5%. Channels are strongly cross-correlated (CCmax 0.53–0.72), and a Hilbert-transform phase lag grows with energy separation, with low energies leading. The full event-by-event catalogue (Table 2) covers flares that overlap the Solar Orbiter era, which makes it a ready-made comparison set for STIX. However, the analysed band is mostly thermal for X-class flares, there is no imaging and no spectral fitting, and the paper's claims about non-thermal electrons rest on interpretation rather than demonstration.

Same scientific problem4×0.30

It addresses QPPs in flare X-ray emission, their energy dependence, and inter-band timing as clues to how electrons are modulated, which is the core of the QPP and energy-dependent-timing theme. It stops at disk-integrated light curves, with no spatial origin and no spectral evolution.

Same methodology4×0.25

The pipeline (EEMD, Morlet wavelet with AR(1) significance, a significance-masked modulation depth, cross-correlation and Hilbert phase) is Python-based (PyEMD) and could be applied directly to STIX quicklook or science light curves. Some choices are methodologically weak (see caveats), so it is a template to improve on rather than adopt as is.

Same data / instruments2×0.15

The primary data are HEL1OS, not STIX or EUI; GOES/XRS is the only shared context instrument. The events, however, fall in 2024–2026, when STIX was operating, so many are likely to have STIX coverage.

Same physical phenomenon4×0.20

Same phenomenon: quasi-periodic modulation of flare HXR emission and its link to electron acceleration and reconnection. The physics is weakened because 8–20 keV in X-class flares is largely thermal, so the 'non-thermal' interpretation is not established.

Potential future utility4×0.10

Table 2 gives dates, times, periods, modulation depths and lags for 34 X-class flares that can be cross-matched with STIX and EUI. Its energy-dependence and phase-lag claims are testable hypotheses that STIX's wider energy range (above 25 keV) and imaging could confirm or refute.

Evidence

The QPP period is essentially the same in all four bands: mean 1.67, 1.64, 1.71 and 1.59 min for 8–11 to 17–20 keV (Table 1, Fig. 4a). Distributions peak at 1.4–1.6 min (Fig. 3a), and 80% of periods lie between 1 and 3 min (Section 6).

Inference

If real, an energy-independent ~90 s period points to a common driver acting on the whole emitting population. However, the tabulated periods are quantised on a discrete grid (1.09, 1.16, 1.23, 1.30, 1.38, 1.46, 1.55, 1.64, 1.74, 1.84 ...), so wavelet scale resolution limits precision. The selection of a single 'clearest' IMF (often IMF2 for every band) could also impose a common timescale through EMD's dyadic filter-bank behaviour.

Recommendation

When replicating with STIX, measure periods with methods that are not tied to one IMF index, such as Fourier/Bayesian fitting of the full power spectrum or EMD-specific significance tests. Check whether the 1.3–1.7 min clustering survives a change of cadence, since the IMF timescale scales with sampling.

Evidence

Modulation depth increases with energy, from 1.29% to 3.82% (Table 1, Fig. 4c). Correlations are Pearson r=0.48 and Spearman ρ=0.54, and a power law with α≈1.4–1.6 is fitted to the four channel means (R²=0.991; Section 6). GOES depths are 0.36–0.48% (Section 5.7).

Inference

The trend matches an increasing non-thermal fraction with energy in a mostly thermal 8–20 keV spectrum. The paper does not do the spectral fitting needed to tell whether the electron population itself is modulated or whether a constant non-thermal modulation is simply diluted less by thermal emission at higher energy. The R² comes from four averaged points and overstates the strength of the result.

Recommendation

Repeat the depth–energy analysis with STIX out to 25–80 keV and pair it with time-resolved spectral fits (thermal plus thick-target), so modulation can be attributed to thermal or non-thermal components. Compare the depth of fitted non-thermal parameters, not just count-rate depth.

Evidence

Phase difference between bands grows with energy separation: 6° for (8–11,11–14), 23° for (8–11,14–17) and 37° for (8–11,17–20) keV, with low energies leading. Scatter is large, and individual distributions overlap zero (Section 5.6, Fig. 5b). Time lags are mostly 0 within a stated 10 s resolution.

Inference

At a ~90 s period, 37° is about 9 s, which is at the stated time-resolution limit; the sign (low energy first) is opposite to simple time-of-flight expectations. It may reflect thermal/non-thermal mixing or cooling, not electron transport. This is the energy-dependent timing signature the STIX-based work could resolve with sub-second to few-second cadence.

Recommendation

Treat the phase-lag result as a hypothesis. Test it on the overlapping events with STIX at its native cadence, extended above 25 keV, and check whether the lag sign flips once the thermal component dominates or is removed.

medium confidence If building a STIX QPP statistic, report results from at least two detection methods. Cite Broomhall et al. (2019) and Hayes et al. (2020)…
Evidence

QPP detection rate is 25/34 (~74%) and shows no trend with GOES class. Strong flares such as SOL2024-10-03 X9.0 and SOL2024-10-01 X7.1 show QPPs, while SOL2025-11-11 X5.1 and SOL2026-02-01 X8.1 do not (Section 5.2, Table 2). The authors note that Hayes et al. (2020) found 46% and attribute the difference to their method.

Inference

Detection rate depends on the method. Testing narrowband IMFs against an AR(1) red-noise model tends to favour detection, so the 74% may be an upper estimate rather than a physical occurrence rate.

Recommendation

If building a STIX QPP statistic, report results from at least two detection methods. Cite Broomhall et al. (2019) and Hayes et al. (2020) for method-dependence, and use this paper's no-QPP events as test cases.

Evidence

The flares span 2024-09-12 to 2026-02-04 with exact GOES times (Table 2). They include large events such as SOL2024-10-03 X9.0, SOL2024-09-14 X4.5, SOL2025-11-14 X4.0 and SOL2026-02-01 X8.1.

Inference

Any of these visible from Solar Orbiter would allow an independent STIX (and possibly EUI) check of HEL1OS QPP periods, plus a spatial study of where the modulation arises in ribbons and loops. Solar Orbiter visibility for each event is not established by the paper.

Recommendation

Cross-match Table 2 with the STIX flare list and the EUI/Major Flare Campaign windows. Prioritise high-Q events (e.g. SOL2025-11-04 X1.8, Q up to ~19) and high-depth events (SOL2025-06-17 X1.2, SOL2025-03-28 X1.1) for joint case studies.

Caveats7The 8–20 keV range in X-class flares is usually dominated by thermal emission, yet the paper calls the 17–20…
  • The 8–20 keV range in X-class flares is usually dominated by thermal emission, yet the paper calls the 17–20 keV channel 'non-thermal' without spectral fitting. Conclusions about modulation of accelerated electrons are therefore interpretive.
  • Several internal inconsistencies exist. The abstract says X1.1–X7.1 while the table includes X9.0 and X8.1. Dates appear as both July 2024–March 2026 and September 2024–February 2026. Cadence is stated as 1 s in Section 2 but 10 s in Section 5.6. Fig. 4 panel descriptions do not match the text, and Eq. (1) is written as a sum rather than an RMS average.
  • IMF selection ('clearest and most coherent') is subjective. Testing an already narrowband IMF against an AR(1) red-noise background likely inflates significance, so detection rate and preferential period may be partly methodological.
  • The power-law depth–energy fit (R²=0.991) uses only four channel means with large standard deviations; event-level correlations are moderate (r≈0.5).
  • Phase lags (~37° ≈ 9 s at ~90 s period) are at the stated temporal resolution and show large scatter, so the energy-ordered lag is tentative.
  • The data are disk-integrated with no imaging, so the paper cannot say where in ribbons or loops the modulation arises.
  • This is an arXiv draft, likely not yet peer-reviewed; reference duplications (Hayes 2020a/b, Simões 2015a/b, Nakariakov & Melnikov 2009a/b are the same papers) suggest it has not been fully proofread.

Abstract screen 9/10: Catalogue and statistical study of hard X-ray QPPs in X-class flares using EEMD and wavelet analysis, with energy-dependent periods, modulation depth and phase coherence. This closely matches the QPP and X-ray timing goals, and the methods transfer directly to STIX lightcurves, though the instrument is Aditya-L1/HEL1OS rather than STIX/EUI.
