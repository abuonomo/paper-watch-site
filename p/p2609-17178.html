8/10abstract3.0/5full text Count-based spectral component imaging (CCI) of solar flares in X-rays

Alessia Guidetti, Muriel Zoë Stiefel, Paolo Massa, Anna Maria Massone et al.
Submitted 15 Sep 2026, astro-ph.SR
method relation arXiv:2609.17178PDFADS

Introduces Count-based spectral Component Imaging (CCI), which uses Richardson-Lucy inversion of STIX pixel counts to jointly reconstruct hot and superhot emission-measure maps and a non-thermal electron-flux map. It is demonstrated on two 40 s intervals of the X7.1 flare SOL2024-10-01T22 and agrees with the earlier SCI method and with spatially integrated spectral fits.

The paper models the flare photon spectrum at each pixel as two isothermal components plus thick-target emission. Temperatures, Ec and δ are fixed from a spatially integrated spectral fit. This gives a forward model (Eq. 8) that is linear in the unknown maps EM_H(x), EM_S(x) and F(x). The model is discretised as C = GMK + B (Eq. 9) and solved directly from STIX counts with a Poisson-appropriate Richardson-Lucy iteration (Eq. 10). This removes SCI's intermediate step of solving for per-component visibilities and its need for total EM and flux as inputs. Applied to the non-thermal peak (22:08:02–22:08:42) and the thermal peak (22:12:02–22:12:42) of SOL2024-10-01T22 (Fig. 1), CCI reproduces SCI morphologies for the hot (~26 MK), superhot (~45–47 MK) and non-thermal sources (Fig. 2). Totals from summing pixels above a 5% contour match the spectral fits within ~10%, except for superhot EM in time range 1, which differs by 18% (Table 1). For the researcher, this is a tool for assigning spectral components to spatial structures (footpoints vs loops) within STIX data. It is not a timing or QPP study, and it is shown only for two snapshots of one event.

The paper addresses spatially separating thermal, superhot and non-thermal X-ray components, which bears on locating accelerated electrons. It does not study temporal evolution, energy-dependent timing or QPPs.

Same methodology3×0.25

STIX spectral fitting with joint background and imaging detectors, plus count-based RL imaging, are directly usable in STIX flare analysis. The method could be run in a time series to track source-resolved non-thermal flux, but the paper does not do so.

Same data / instruments5×0.15

Uses Solar Orbiter/STIX data for an X7.1 Cycle 25 flare, with the attenuator inserted. This is exactly the instrument and dataset class of interest.

Same physical phenomenon3×0.20

The paper treats non-thermal electron flux maps (thick-target) and hot/superhot flare plasma, which are core flare energetics and acceleration phenomena. Dynamics and variability are not addressed.

Potential future utility3×0.10

Applied over successive time bins, CCI could yield component-resolved lightcurves separating footpoint non-thermal sources from superhot coronal sources. That would help attribute pulsations to specific structures for comparison with EUI. This is an inference, not demonstrated.

medium confidence For case-study flares where intermediate-energy lightcurves show variability, use CCI or SCI component maps rather than energy-band images…
Evidence

Fig. 2 shows that the 20–22 keV energy-band image in time range 1 mixes thermal and non-thermal structures. CCI separates these into a single compact hot source, a two-lobed superhot source and two non-thermal footpoint-like sources.

Inference

When relating STIX timing signatures to EUI ribbons or loops, energy-band images can misattribute emission in the ~15–30 keV range. Component imaging gives a cleaner spatial assignment of the non-thermal electrons.

Recommendation

For case-study flares where intermediate-energy lightcurves show variability, use CCI or SCI component maps rather than energy-band images to decide whether the variable emission is footpoint (non-thermal) or coronal (superhot).

Evidence

CCI takes TH, TS, Ec and δ from a spatially integrated spectral fit as fixed inputs. Totals are outputs. Sensitivity to the inputs is assessed with 100 Gaussian-perturbed reconstructions (Sect. 2.1, 3.2).

Inference

The method assumes a single spectral shape per component across the whole source. Spatial variation in δ or temperature between footpoints, or over time during pulsations, is not captured.

Recommendation

If applying CCI time-resolved, refit spectra for each time bin and be cautious interpreting differences in footpoint flux ratio. Spectral hardness differences between sources would need separate imaging spectroscopy.

Evidence

Each CCI reconstruction integrates 40 s, during attenuator insertion, for an X7.1 flare (Sect. 3, Fig. 1). Table 1 totals agree with spectral fits to within ~10% at the 5% threshold.

Inference

Photon statistics here are extremely high. Whether CCI remains stable at the short integrations needed to resolve QPPs (seconds) or for weaker M-class flares is not shown.

Recommendation

Before adopting CCI for time-series studies, test it on shorter intervals and fainter events, using the paper's Monte Carlo uncertainty scheme to quantify degradation.

medium confidence Consider this event for time-resolved STIX/EUI analysis, reusing its published spectral parameters as a starting point.
Evidence

Fig. 1 shows a strongly impulsive 32–76 keV peak near 22:08, a secondary bump near 22:09:30 and an earlier precursor near 22:05, while the 5–15 keV emission rises smoothly to a peak near 22:12.

Inference

SOL2024-10-01T22 has multiple non-thermal bursts and a well-separated thermal peak. Combined with existing STIX spectral fits and MHD modelling (Matsumoto et al.), it is a well-documented candidate case study for timing analysis.

Recommendation

Consider this event for time-resolved STIX/EUI analysis, reusing its published spectral parameters as a starting point.

Caveats6Proof of concept on a single event and only two 40 s snapshots, with no time series or statistical validation.
  • Proof of concept on a single event and only two 40 s snapshots, with no time series or statistical validation.
  • Validation is against SCI and spectral fits that share the same input parameters, so this is consistency rather than independent ground truth.
  • The isothermal (delta-function DEM) and spatially uniform δ/Ec assumptions may hide physically interesting spatial spectral variation.
  • Albedo is fitted spectrally but neglected in imaging.
  • STIX dynamic range (~1:20) limits faint-source recovery, and totals depend on the chosen contour threshold.
  • No discussion of timing, QPPs or EUI co-alignment; relevance to those aims is inferred.
