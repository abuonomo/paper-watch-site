A New Framework for Modeling Solar Flares from MHD to Kinetic Processes

Joel C. Allred, Graham S. Kerr, Silvina E. Guidoni, Joel T. Dahlin et al.
Submitted 1 Oct 2026, astro-ph.SR
21 pages, 12 figures, accepted for publication by Astrophysical Journal
arXivPDFADS

Chains the 2.5D ARMS MHD eruption model, the kglobal reconnection-acceleration model and RADYN+FP to inject self-consistent electron and proton distributions into a single newly reconnected flare loop, then follows transport, return currents, mirroring, chromospheric evaporation and condensation, white-light emission and HXR source height.

The authors take the flare current sheet from an ultra-high-resolution 2.5D ARMS breakout eruption (the Guidoni et al. 2016 run). They use its upstream conditions to run kglobal, which yields electron and proton spectra with κ≈2.9 (Fig. 2), most acceleration occurring within about 24 s. They inject these into a RADYN+FP loop built from an ARMS reconnected field line. Injection follows a leaky-box exponential decay with τi=20 s, chosen explicitly to match typical QPP periods (Hayes et al. 2020). FP is extended to transport multiple species coupled through the return current. Results include: - a ~24–28 MK looptop flash from return-current Ohmic heating in the first ~1 s; - evaporation upflows falling from 1.7 Mm/s to 160 km/s; - condensation of 10–30 km/s; - protons undergoing warm-target collisions in the corona and stopping sharply at the transition region; - pitch-angle-dependent electron fates under return-current, collisional and mirroring forces (Figs. 10–11); - co-spatial 30–50 keV HXR emissivity and white-light enhancement about 690 km above the photosphere, matching Krucker et al. (2015) limb observations. For STIX/EUI timing work, it is a physics-context and forward-modelling resource rather than an observational or time-series analysis. It models only a single injection episode in one symmetric loop.

AbstractPhysical processes in solar flares span many orders of magnitude in spatiotemporal scales, so a single unified model ac…

Physical processes in solar flares span many orders of magnitude in spatiotemporal scales, so a single unified model accounting for all relevant processes remains computationally intractable. Instead, specialized codes have been developed, targeting specific domains that tackle specific aspects of the solar flare problem. Here, we present a comprehensive framework that links three models to capture flare initiation and energy release, the subsequent particle acceleration, the propagation and thermalization of those particles, and ultimately the heating of the lower solar atmosphere. We use the 2.5D ARMS magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) code to model stressing of the magnetic field in a simulated active region, the formation of a current sheet, magnetic reconnection, and the evolution of newly formed flare loops. We use the conditions in the ARMS' current sheet to initialize the kglobal model, which predicts time-dependent nonthermal electron and proton distributions. Finally, we use RADYN+FP to inject these distributions into a newly reconnected ARMS loop and compute subsequent particle transport, atmospheric heating, and radiative emissions. This is the first detailed presentation of a RADYN+FP simulation tracking nonthermal electrons and protons in the same loop. As well as discussing the interesting physics that takes place, we note how this results in white-light flare emission height consistent with observations. This work is a first step toward building a fully 3D flare modeling framework.

  • Self-consistently derived electron and proton spectra from reconnection, κ≈2.9 for both, with acceleration timescale ~5τA≈24 s (Sec. 3.2, Fig. 2). A reference for the acceleration timescale and spectral hardness to compare with STIX-derived indices.
  • Time-dependent loop response to an exponentially decaying (τi=20 s) beam (Figs. 4–6): looptop 24–28 MK within ~1.4 s, evaporation reaching the apex at 7.3 s, apex density and temperature at 10, 20 and 40 s. Useful for sanity-checking thermal/nonthermal timing in STIX lightcurves.
  • Quantitative breakdown of return-current versus collisional versus mirroring losses for 20 keV electrons at 3° and 45° pitch angles (Figs. 9–11). Return-current losses of ~2.5 keV occur early and collisional losses of ~9 keV in the corona at t=5 s, which matters for interpreting low-energy spectral flattening and energy-dependent delays.
  • Synthetic 30–50 keV HXR emissivity height (~690 km above photosphere) co-spatial with white-light emission (Fig. 12, Sec. 3.3.2). An alternative explanation for low HXR footpoint heights invoking chromospheric compression rather than low ambient densities.
  • Temperature-dependent upflow/downflow crossover at 1–2 MK (Fig. 8), relevant to EUV ribbon/loop Doppler and intensity interpretation.

high confidence Use the single-pulse response (heating rise in <1 s, evaporation filling in ~7–10 s) as a reference when deconvolving multi-pulse STIX ligh…
Evidence

Sec. 3.3: the injection flux decays exponentially with τi=20 s, justified by QPP intervals of 'typically about 20 s' (Hayes et al. 2020). QPPs are described as potential signatures of individual reconnection bursts (Ashfield et al. 2026). Sec. 4 lists this as an assumption and notes that only one injection episode is modelled.

Inference

The model treats one QPP-like burst as one injection episode, but it does not produce or test QPPs. Its value for QPP work is as a template for a single pulse's thermal and nonthermal response, not as evidence for any QPP mechanism.

Recommendation

Use the single-pulse response (heating rise in <1 s, evaporation filling in ~7–10 s) as a reference when deconvolving multi-pulse STIX lightcurves. Do not cite this paper as support for a QPP mechanism.

Evidence

Sec. 3.3 and Figs. 10–11, 9: in the first ~0.5 s the return-current electric field exceeds Dreicer at the looptop. Electrons lose ~2.5 keV to return-current losses in the corona, and high-pitch-angle electrons are reversed. Later, collisional losses dominate (9 keV at t=5 s), and after ~25 s high-pitch-angle electrons are thermalized in the corona before returning.

Inference

Transport effects evolve on second timescales during a single injection. Energy-dependent timing and spectral changes seen by STIX could therefore partly reflect evolving return-current and collisional conditions in the loop rather than changes at the accelerator.

Recommendation

When interpreting STIX spectral flattening at low energies or energy-dependent delays early in impulsive bursts, consider return-current and coronal-density evolution as alternatives to acceleration changes. Cross-reference Alaoui et al. 2021 and Allred et al. 2020.

medium confidence If analysing near-limb STIX/EUI events, compare footpoint heights against this prediction and against Krucker et al. 2015.
Evidence

Sec. 3.3.2, Fig. 12 right: model 30–50 keV HXR emissivity peaks at the base of the compressed transition region, ~690 km above the photosphere, co-spatial with the white-light enhancement. The authors argue this matches Krucker et al. (2015) without requiring low footpoint densities.

Inference

This bears on interpreting HXR footpoint heights and HXR–EUV/white-light co-spatiality. It is relevant if STIX and EUI limb or near-limb events are used to locate where emission originates in ribbons.

Recommendation

If analysing near-limb STIX/EUI events, compare footpoint heights against this prediction and against Krucker et al. 2015.

Evidence

Sec. 3.2, Fig. 2: kglobal with guide field 0.3 gives κ≈2.9 for both species. The authors note this is harder than typical solar flare spectra and that the guide field strongly controls nonthermal production (Dahlin et al. 2014; Arnold et al. 2021).

Inference

The guide-field dependence offers a physical lever linking observed STIX spectral indices to reconnection geometry. The specific spectrum here is not representative of typical events.

Recommendation

Treat the kglobal spectrum as illustrative only. For linking observed spectral indices to reconnection, read Arnold et al. 2021 and Yin et al. 2024a.

Caveats6Pure simulation: no STIX, EUI or any observational data are analysed, so it offers no direct dataset, event o…
  • Pure simulation: no STIX, EUI or any observational data are analysed, so it offers no direct dataset, event or analysis technique.
  • Only one injection episode into one symmetric loop from a 2.5D model. No lightcurves, QPPs or multi-loop ribbon evolution are produced, so temporal-variability predictions are limited.
  • The 20 s injection decay time is assumed from QPP statistics, not derived. The paper does not test QPP mechanisms.
  • The ARMS active region is idealized (fields ~2.5 G, size ×10, rescaled by factors of 10 and 4). The kglobal spectrum (κ≈2.9) is harder than typical flare spectra.
  • The FP solver is time-independent per snapshot and omits runaway-electron return current, so early-time (<0.5 s) return-current losses are overestimated by the authors' own admission.
  • Synthetic HXR is shown only as an emissivity height profile (30–50 keV). No synthetic spectra or count lightcurves suitable for direct STIX comparison are given.
