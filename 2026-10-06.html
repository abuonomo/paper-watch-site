6 Exploring an X1.8 flare and associated quasi-periodic pulsations using SDO and Aditya-L1 observations, alongside NFFF extrapolation

Pawan Kumar, Sanjay Kumar, Sadashiv, Sachin Rajkapoor Kanaujiya et al. · submitted 2026-10-06 direct
21 pages, 9 figures accepted for publication in Solar Physics
arXiv:2610.08124PDFADS

Case study of the X1.8 flare SOL2024-10-09T01:24 (AR 13848). It detects ~4-min QPPs in the HEL1OS/Aditya-L1 20–50 keV lightcurve, localises them with STIX MEM_GE imaging and AIA ribbon lightcurves to one ribbon (R1), and interprets them via NFFF-extrapolated null/flux-rope/QSL topology.

The authors combine GOES-18 SXR, AIA EUV/UV imaging, HEL1OS 20–50 keV HXR lightcurves (2 s bins), a single STIX MEM_GE image, and an NFFF extrapolation of the HMI SHARP magnetogram at 01:24 UT. Method: they detrend the HXR lightcurve with a Savitzky–Golay filter (251 s window), then apply a Morlet wavelet (Torrence & Compo) and an FFT periodogram tested against an AR(1) red-noise 95% level. Both give a period of ~4 min (4.14 min FFT, 3.94 min global wavelet) during the impulsive phase, ~01:35–01:50 UT. AIA 171 Å and 1600 Å lightcurves of four ribbons/brightenings show that peaks from ribbon R1 roughly coincide with the HXR peaks. A STIX 20–50 keV image (01:42:00–01:42:30 UT), reprojected to the Earth view and manually co-aligned, overlaps R1. The extrapolation shows a flux rope (|Tw|>1), a 3D null, a sheared arcade and four QSLs. The authors propose that recurrent reconnection at the null and in the arcade drives both flare onset and the QPPs, but they acknowledge this cannot explain the period itself.

Connections

  • Directly on topic for QPPs in flare HXR and their spatial localisation in ribbons. It follows the Clarke et al. 2021 and Collier et al. 2024 approach of correlating ribbon UV/EUV lightcurves with HXR pulsations (Fig. 6a).
  • Uses STIX data through stixpy with MEM_GE imaging and SunPy reprojection to the Earth view (Sect. 2.2, Fig. 6b). This is the same toolchain the PhD will use, though applied only minimally here.
  • Supplies a candidate event (2024-10-09 X1.8) whose STIX timing data were not analysed. STIX's own lightcurves and spectra could be used to test the 4-min QPP and its energy dependence.
  • The QPP pipeline (SG detrending → wavelet + FFT with red-noise test) is a common baseline. It is also an example of the detrending pitfalls (Inglis/Hayes, Broomhall et al.) the researcher should avoid or improve on.

Possible applications

  • Re-analyse SOL2024-10-09 X1.8 with STIX quicklook and science lightcurves (4 s or better cadence, multiple energy bands). This would confirm or refute the 4-min period without detrending, e.g. with a Fourier power-law + Lorentzian fit or the Inglis & Hayes 2024 method, and would test energy-dependent timing and thermal/nonthermal separation.
  • Check Solar Orbiter's position and whether EUI/FSI or HRI observed AR 13848 on 2024-10-09, to localise pulsations in EUV at higher cadence than AIA, which saturated.
  • Do time-resolved STIX imaging across individual pulses rather than a single 30 s window, to see whether footpoints migrate between R1 and other ribbons from pulse to pulse.
  • Run time-resolved STIX spectral fitting (thermal + thick-target) per pulse to track whether the spectral index hardens or softens through the QPPs.
  • Cross-compare HEL1OS and STIX lightcurves for this event as a cross-calibration and timing check.

Caveats

  • The QPP detection is weak methodologically. A 251 s SG detrending window is close to the claimed ~240 s period, which risks artificially imposing or suppressing that period. The pulsations shown are dominated by 2–3 large spikes rather than a clear train of more than three pulses.
  • No energy-resolved timing, no spectral analysis, and only one energy band (20–50 keV) are used.
  • STIX is used only for a single 30 s image with manual co-alignment. STIX lightcurves and spectroscopy are not exploited, and EUI is not used at all.
  • The EUV/UV–HXR correlation is qualitative ('nearly coincident' peaks) with no cross-correlation coefficients or lags. The 171 Å channel is used because other AIA channels saturated.
  • The link between the extrapolated topology and the QPPs is speculative, and the extrapolation is a single static pre-flare snapshot. The authors admit it cannot account for the period.
  • Data are available only on request.
